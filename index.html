About this demo

The straight skeleton of a polygon is traced by its corners while every edge moves inward, parallel to itself, at constant speed. Corners stay sharp (a mitered offset), so at a reflex corner the offset keeps a pointed shape. A rounded offset, as in a Minkowski or buffer offset, would instead swing an arc of circle around it.

Height = arrival time of the wavefront, times tan(slope). The result is a hip roof with all planes at the same slope; each face is one roof plane, belonging to one footprint edge. Skeleton arcs are hips and ridges (convex) or valleys (reflex).

The nearest-segment mode shows something different: each point is coloured by its closest boundary element (an open edge, or a reflex vertex in grey), and the height is the true distance to the boundary. Its boundaries are the medial axis (Voronoi diagram of the segments): parabolic arcs next to reflex corners and circular, round cones around reflex vertices, which is the rounded counterpart of the mitered skeleton. It is sampled on a grid here, not computed exactly.
